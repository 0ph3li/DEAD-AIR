# DEAD AIR

**for the platforms we loved and the accounts we lost.**

*funeral directors for the digitally deceased. family owned since 2009. open 24/7 — we never log off.*

`HTML` · `CSS` · `vanilla JS` · `GSAP` · `Lenis` · `Canvas` · `Web Audio` · `real .ics files`

</div>

---

**DEAD AIR** is a concept website for a business that doesn't exist: an elegant, family-run funeral home that holds funerals for things that died online. The old profile, the group chat muted since 2019, the high-school blog, a corrupted save file, a virtual pet forgotten over a long weekend, the phone left in a taxi. It's run by **Vera Vale**, funeral director (long silver nails, no jokes about the cloud), her nephew **Dez**, restorer of the deceased (writes the eulogies, does the playlist), and **Cache**, a crow who collects dead USB sticks.

The whole site looks like a **photocopy of a funeral**: cold paper, grain everywhere, giant condensed black type that drifts in and out of focus, photos printed in halftone, tiny technical captions in mono, one elegant script and a single colour, **lilac**. Every page is a different room of the house (the chapel, the price list, the newspaper, the arrangements office, the florist, the guestbook, the support group) and every page has something to *do*.

## ✦ The pages

### 00 · Loader
A heart monitor on black. The line beats while the counter goes to **100** — *lowering the lights, closing all tabs, folding the programs, arranging the lilies, clearing the cache, please be seated* — then it goes **flat**, *rest in peace*, and the screen splits in two like a pair of doors. The same doors close and open between every page.

### 01 · Home: *in loving memory*
- **The name**: DEAD AIR in huge condensed letters that **focus only where your cursor is**, like a lens; a lilac ghost of the title moves the other way, TV static hums behind it, a framed profile picture and a lily lean on top, and *in loving memory* writes itself across. Scroll and the name goes out of focus, like a memory.
- **Today at the house**: the order of service on a **split-flap board**. The statuses (*scheduled, doors open, in progress, concluded, delayed · buffering*) follow your real clock.
- **Who we are**: a manifesto that comes into focus word by word, with taped strips of text on the wall.
- **Three ways to go**: hover a card and it happens. **Cremation**: a file burns to pixel ash, bottom up, and leaves *0 KB*. **Burial**: a folder sinks six folders deep. **Viking**: a factory reset fills to 100% and the screen says *hello*.
- **Obituaries**: a newspaper page; photos load tile by tile like it's 2006, and every obituary has a **candle** you can light.
- **A moment of silence**: **hold** (or press **F**) for five seconds. The rest of the site goes grey, the heartbeat slows down to a flatline, and *thank you.*
- **The Vale family**: Vera, Dez, and Cache, who caws when you click him.
- **Florist**, **Guestbook** (a 2005 chat window with nudges) and **Plan**: a headstone that engraves itself while you type.

### 02 · Services: *the price list*
- **The list**: Cremation, Burial, Viking. Hover a row and the others blur, while a framed photo **follows the cursor**.
- **Cremation**: the section pins and the word **CREMATION**, drawn in pixels, burns as you scroll. The file size goes from *2.40 MB* to *0 KB*, the four steps light up (*arrival, the viewing, the fire, the urn*) and the urn appears at the end.
- **Burial**: the cemetery scrolls **sideways**. Four arched plots (*the unindexed folder, the hard drive in a drawer, cold storage, the mausoleum*) lean with your scroll speed and rise out of the ground.
- **Viking**: a phone counts down to zero (*12,402 photos, 2,301 drafts…*) while a **fire** grows from the bottom of the screen. Then the welcome screen: *hello, ciao, hola, こんにちは…*
- **Which goodbye?**: three questions, one recommendation (*"it's late. book it tomorrow."*).
- **Little extras**: a menu with dotted leaders — *a eulogy written by Dez*, *read receipts turned off forever*, *Cache the crow attends*. They follow you to the plan page.
- **FAQ**: questions people ask at 3 AM.

### 03 · Obituaries: *in memoriam*
- **The photocopy**: a xerox collage with a **square loupe** that follows the cursor and shows the image zoomed and inverted (click: ×1.8 / ×3.2).
- **The index**: on black, like the back of a record. Ten departed laid out like a tracklist, with the words of each name spread across the line and the years where the track lengths would be. Hover a name and the others blur while its photo rises behind the list, grainy. Filter by kind.
- **The reading room**: the full obituary over a huge blurred initial with a pink haze, a candle, previous / next (arrow keys too), and a link of its own (`obituaries.html#o-blog`).
- **Obituary of the month**: one image mirrored top and bottom, a black band of text across the middle. The halves drift apart as you scroll.
- **Last words**: the last message each of them ever sent, printed on **strips of tape** you can drag around the wall.
- **Write one**: giant blurred letters **R I P** that come into focus *the more you write*. Publish it and it joins the index.

### 04 · Plan a Funeral: *goodbye, properly.*
- **Six steps**: the deceased, the service, where & when, the details, extras, review. Whatever you chose elsewhere (the headstone on the home, the quiz, the extras) is already filled in.
- **The program**: a printed funeral program beside the form that **writes itself** as you go: name, years, epitaph, the casket, the order of service, the room and the date.
- **Where & when**: five rooms (the Dock is for Vikings only) and a 14-day calendar where **other people's funerals** have already taken some of the slots.
- **The details**: four caskets shaped like windows (*the Window, the Terminal, the Notification, the Polaroid*), four kinds of music **you can listen to first** (*chapel organ, a dial-up hymn, an old ringtone slowed down, dead air*), and the florist's flowers.
- **Review**: a torn receipt and a checkbox: *nothing will be recovered. not even if I ask nicely at 3 AM.*
- **Booked**: the screen goes black, the program comes out of a **printer**, line by line, the heart monitor slows down to a flatline. Print the program, or **add it to your calendar** with a real `.ics` (with a reminder an hour before: *bring tissues*).

### 05 · Florist: *say it with lilies*
- **The fan**: five flowers open like a hand of cards over the word FLORIST, petals in the air that your mouse blows around.
- **The language of flowers**: floriography, updated. *White lily: "I won't be logging back in." Black rose: "Seen. Not answered."* The flower in the ring changes as each meaning reaches the middle of the screen.
- **Ready to send**: eight arrangements (*the Unfollow, Seen 3:12 AM, Forever Offline, Left on Read, Typing…*).
- **Make a bouquet**: on black, with the black urn as a vase. Pick from nine stems; each one **drops into the vase**, sways on its own, and the whole bouquet leans toward your cursor. Shuffle it, empty it, write the card, and send it: the envelope seals and flies away, and the tracking reads *delivered · read? never. that's okay.*

### 06 · Guestbook: *sign it for anyone, or anything, you've lost*
- **The book**: an open guestbook with ruled cream pages and fourteen signatures. The pages **turn in 3D**. Sign it and your words **write themselves**, letter by letter, with the scratch of a pen.
- **The messenger**: a 2005 chat client where every contact is one of the departed, all offline, with their old status messages. Write to them: *"is offline. your message will be delivered when they sign in."* Sometimes someone is *typing…* and their **away message** arrives. Sometimes nothing does. Emoticons, and **nudges** that shake the window.
- **The numbers**: signatures, messages, nudges and candles across the whole site.

### 07 · Grief Support: *you don't have to do anything here*
- **Breathe**: a blurred pink orb that breathes, and the title breathes with it. **Breathe with us** starts a guided cycle (in for four, hold, out for six) with a pad that swells and fades.
- **The five stages of digital grief**: pinned to the scroll, one room each. **Denial**: a loading bar stuck at 99%. **Anger**: the word glitches in red and cyan. **Bargaining**: a login that keeps getting the password wrong. **Depression**: 3 AM, old captions raining down. **Acceptance**: *log off.*
- **The 3 AM line**: a phone with **real touch tones**. Dial 1-800-RIP-DATA (or speed dial), it rings, and **Vera picks up** — press 1 if you miss a person, 2 if you miss who you were back then, 3 if you're just tired, 0 for Cache.
- **Support groups**: Unfollowers Anonymous, Left on Read, Former Moderators, Can't Sleep, Still Scrolling. Save a seat.
- **Let it go up**: a night sky full of paper lanterns. Write what you can't let go of, **hold the button**, and watch it rise until you can't see it anymore.
- **Log off**: the whole site fades to black. *goodnight.*

And a real note at the bottom: if someone reading is going through something heavy, a link to a free helpline directory.

## ✦ Everywhere in the house

- **The doors**: every internal link closes two black halves over the page, one heartbeat crosses the line, and the next page opens.
- **The chapel organ**: a switch in the header plays four slow chords on synthesised drawbars, with tremolo and a little echo for the nave. It ducks when the house goes quiet.
- **The crosshair**: the cursor is a printer's registration mark.
- **Respects paid today**: press **F** on any page.
- **Grain**: the whole site is a photocopy, all the time.

## ✦ How it works

- **The heart monitor.** One small class (`DA.Ecg`) draws a sweeping ECG on a canvas from a sum of gaussians (P, QRS, T). `amp` 1 is alive, `amp` 0 is a flatline; GSAP tweens it. The loader, the page doors, the moment of silence and the booking screen all use it.
- **Fire, ash and focus.** The cremation effects sample a word or a file icon drawn on an offscreen canvas into thousands of pixels, then send each one up as an ember and then as lilac ash, bottom first. The Viking fire is a particle system that grows with your scroll. The hero lens is a per-letter `filter: blur()` driven by the distance from the cursor.
- **Pinned scenes.** Cremation, the burial plots, the Viking reset and the five stages are GSAP ScrollTriggers with `pin` and `scrub`. The burial plots use `containerAnimation` to rise as they cross the screen.
- **One state, every page.** The headstone on the home, the quiz on Services and the extras all write to the same plan, so the Plan page opens already filled in. Candles are shared between the home and the obituaries.
- **Sound in code.** The organ, the heartbeat beeps, the flatline, the crackle of the fire, the touch tones (real DTMF frequencies), the ringback, the dial-up hymn, the slowed-down ringtone (a public-domain waltz), the nudge, the crow and the breathing pad are all synthesised with the **Web Audio API**: no audio files.
- **A real calendar file.** The booking downloads a standard `.ics` with a one-hour reminder, built in the browser.
- **Image slots.** Every photo is a slot with `data-img`. Drop a file with the right name in `img/<PAGE>/` and it appears; until then a labelled placeholder shows what goes there. Photos are printed in halftone by CSS, so any photo works.
- **Cutouts.** Flowers, the crow, the urn and the phone were cut out with a small Python script (flood-fill the background from the edges, soften the mask, crop) and saved as **WebP**. The originals are kept in `img/_orig/`.
- **Still mode.** Add `?still` to any address and all motion is switched off, with every animation in its finished state. Handy for screenshots.

## ✦ Privacy

Nothing leaves the browser. There is no server, no analytics and no tracking. The obituaries you write, your signatures, your messages to the departed, the candles, your plan, your bouquet and your lanterns are kept on your device in `localStorage` (keys start with `da-`). Nobody else can read them. Not even Vera.

## ✦ Run it locally

There is no build step. Serve the folder with any static server:

```bash
python3 -m http.server 5190
```

then open <http://localhost:5190>.

## ✦ Built with

- Plain **HTML, CSS and JavaScript**: no framework, no build step
- [GSAP 3](https://gsap.com/) + ScrollTrigger · [Lenis](https://github.com/darkroomengineering/lenis) for smooth scrolling
- Canvas 2D · Web Audio API · Intersection Observer
- Google Fonts: Archivo (variable width), Instrument Serif, Pinyon Script, DM Mono, Silkscreen

Designed for desktop and mobile. Respects `prefers-reduced-motion`, with a skip link, visible focus and keyboard access throughout (the strips on the wall move with the arrow keys, the reading room closes with Esc, the phone dials from the keyboard).
